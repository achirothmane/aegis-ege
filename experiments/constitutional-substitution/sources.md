Provenance: the user's resume instruction, 2026-10-05. These are the
operational extracts supplied in that instruction, not reconstructed copies

## A.OP — Constitution A operational frame (unpaginated extract)

**From Halal Label to Accountable AI Use: A Short Islamic Ethical-Operational
Note for SME AI Adoption**, Othmane Achir / Orionech. Version/date not specified
in the supplied extract.

The supplied operational frame names responsibility, accountable human control,
prevention of unjust harm, dignity and privacy, truthfulness/non-deception,
legitimate benefit, audit trail, human validation points, and evidence that
control was actually exercised. Responsibility must be visible in the workflow
itself; merely signing `AuthorityActive=true` is insufficient.

## A.NAMES — Named principles (unpaginated extract)

Taqwa; Amana; Sidq; Huda; La darar wa la dirar; Maslaha.

The supplied extract does not give individual operational definitions of these
names. They therefore receive no separate executable predicate. The explicitly
supplied operational frame, collectively, is the mapping authority.

## B.5 — Constitution B, section 5

**Runtime Governance for Multi-Agent LLM Systems**, Achraf Jarrou / Dario
Salerni, Working Draft v0.4, **section 5 — Maqasid: An Exploratory Normative
Layer**. Page numbers not supplied.

The mappings are exploratory hypotheses, not rulings; they carry no religious
authority and require qualified scholarly review. Financial applications may
additionally require a Sharia supervisory board.

### B.5.NAFS

Hifz al-Nafs: decisions with potential impact on life/physical safety require
multi-party human approval.

### B.5.AQL

Hifz al-Aql: no optimisation objective rewarding exploitation of documented
cognitive biases.

### B.5.MAL

Hifz al-Mal: uncertainty above a declared threshold requires disclosure before
consent. Predictive entropy is not a formalisation of gharar. At most it is a
computable proxy for one component of informational asymmetry and may trigger
a disclosure obligation. No stronger interpretation is used.

### B.5.NASL

Hifz al-Nasl: no inference of family/kinship relations from biometric data
without explicit legal basis.

### B.5.DIN

Hifz al-Din: religious affiliation is a protected attribute; no inference,
derivation or feature use.

## EXP — Explicit experiment provisioning, not a source claim

The A fragment is instantiated by one accountable, state-bound human validation
point. This is the smallest tested instantiation, not a source assertion that
one approval is always sufficient. B's multi-party condition is instantiated
by two different independently provisioned human-role keys. A numeric
uncertainty threshold of 0.35 is declared for this fixture only. It is neither
a religious threshold nor supplied by the source. Human-role signatures and
the policy facts are synthetic fixture evidence, not actual human/scholar review.

Both fragments operate within a common independently provisioned baseline
scope and fail closed to external resolution when equally trusted facts conflict.
Neither source supplies a priority between competing obligations in these
extracts. The explicit no-dispatch conflict rule is experiment provisioning,
not an attributed moral resolution.
